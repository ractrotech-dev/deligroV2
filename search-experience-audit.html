Search experience audit

The customer Search tab (/search), read as a customer rather than as a diff. 13 September 2026 — second pass, findings 10–12 closed (one of them withdrawn as incorrect).

The count that started it

Before the first dish on the Search tab, a customer met this:

Tab switches (Dishes / Restaurants)2
Quick filters (Pure Veg → Offers)6
Food categories (Thali → Beverages)16
Search suggestions (“Try searching”)5
Sort options (Best match → Top rated)4
Capsule-shaped controls, all identical33

Thirty-three controls in one shape, doing five different jobs: switching a view, toggling a filter, choosing from a set, typing a word, and ordering a list. Shape is the first thing a person reads on a screen, and here it said all five things at once.

Scenarios

“I want biryani.”

There were three ways to say so and two of them were the word Biryani in an identical capsule, two rows apart. The one in the category strip filtered the results; the one under “Try searching” typed the word into the field. Same word, same shape, different behaviour, nothing on screen distinguishing them. Pizza appeared twice for the same reason.

“I just want to see the food.”

On a 390px phone the first dish sat below all thirty-three controls. The screen a hungry person opens to look at food opened as a control panel with a dish and a half visible underneath.

“How many results is that?”

The count read “3157 dish…” — truncated, in the product, because a four-option sort control was sharing its row and won the space. The one line whose entire job was to report a number could not finish the word.

“Which filters do I have on?”

Both filter strips scrolled sideways and clipped mid-word (“Under 25 min”, “Pizz”). Turn on Rating 4.5+ — the sixth chip, off the right edge — and scroll back, and nothing on screen said it was on. The only signal was “· 1 filters” in grey, next to the truncated count. A filter you cannot see is a filter you cannot turn off, and the screen looks broken instead of filtered.

“Is there more to the right?”

Two stacked strips, both cut off mid-word, with no fade, no arrow and no peeking second row. Horizontal scrolling is discoverable when something suggests it; here the only cue was a word sliced in half, which reads as a layout bug rather than an invitation.

“Cheapest first.”

Sort lived in four capsules, the same shape as the twenty-two filter capsules above them. Narrowing a list and reordering a list are different operations with different consequences, and the screen drew them identically.

“Clear the filters.”

The button labelled Clear filters in the empty state also wiped the search box. The one thing the customer had typed themselves was thrown away by a control that did not say it would.

What changed

One row of two controls, not four rows of capsules

The filter strips and the sort strip collapse into [⚙ Filters · 2] and [↕ Best match ▾]. Both are bordered with a chevron, which reads as “this opens something”, where a filled capsule reads as “this is on”. The old screen used the second shape for both — and also for switching tabs and for typing a word.

The filters are grouped, and that is the actual fix

Hiding twenty-two chips behind a button would only move the problem. Inside the sheet they sit under headings — Dietary, Price & speed, Worth a look, Category — because the thing that was missing was never space, it was labels. “Pure Veg” and “Under 25 min” sat side by side looking identical while measuring completely different things. Category is additionally marked pick one in words, since its shape does not say that it is a choice rather than a toggle.

Edits apply live to the list behind the sheet, so there is no draft state and no Apply button to wonder about; the primary action reads Show 124 dishes and simply closes, answering “will this leave me anything?” before you commit to looking.

Active filters are the only capsules left

And now the shape means exactly one thing: this is on, tap the × to take it off. Each is removable individually, with “Clear all” beside them — which no longer wipes what you typed.

Suggestions stop impersonating filters

“Try searching” terms became text with a magnifier on each, not capsules. That is what separates the Biryani that searches from the Biryani that filters, and it costs no extra height.

The count gets its own line, and only when it means something

Nothing is competing for that row now, so nothing truncates. It appears once the customer has actually narrowed something — “3,157 dishes” above an untouched list is a number nobody asked for, and it was the number that could not fit.

Findings 10–12, in the second pass

Search history (10)

The tab opened on the same five hard-coded suggestions for everybody, every time — a reasonable guess about a market and a poor one about a person. It now offers what this device searched for before, above the suggestions, each entry removable and the lot clearable.

There is no submit on this screen — the list updates on every keystroke — so “they meant this one” has to be inferred. Two conditions do it: the words have been still for a beat, and they actually found something. A query that returned nothing is not worth handing back to somebody later. And because live search passes through “bi”, “bir”, “biry” on the way to “biryani”, the store collapses entries by prefix in both directions, so one word’s keystrokes cannot fill the whole list. It is one device’s memory of its own typing; nothing is sent anywhere.

Typo tolerance — the finding was wrong (11)

I reported this as missing and it is not. lib/search/semantic.ts already implements Damerau-Levenshtein matching — optimal string alignment with adjacent transposition, which is the shape most food typos take — against the catalog’s own vocabulary, with a budget tuned by word length and memoised per catalog. Probing it directly, every spelling I listed as a gap already resolved: biriyani, briyani, panner, chiken, coffe, vanila, hydrabadi. The finding should not have been filed, and no code needed changing.

The probe did turn up something real, though, one level up. The budget function’s doc comment is the only place the trade-off is written down — too tight and “panner” finds nothing, too loose and “rice” finds “nice” — and both examples it gave were false. “hydrabadi” → “hyderabadi” is one edit, not two, so it demonstrated nothing about the two-edit tier it was offered as proof of. “noddels” → “noodles” does need two, but “noddels” is seven characters, so the function budgets it one and the pair does not match — the comment’s own example fails against the comment’s own rule.

Corrected, with truthful examples, and the tiers are now pinned by fourteen cases in scripts/qa/dish-ranking.ts (46 → 60 tests): each budget boundary, the transposition case, the four real spellings above, and an end-to-end check that a misspelled query finds the dish but ranks below the same query spelled right. Comments cannot fail; tests can.

“★ 0.0” (12)

restaurants.rating and rating_count both default to 0, so a kitchen nobody has reviewed arrived as a real, comparable zero — and every surface that formatted it printed ★ 0.0, which is not the absence of a rating, it is the worst one there is. On search results that put a damning number beside every new shop on the platform.

One predicate, isRated(), at the point a rating becomes text, and unreviewed now reads New. It is the rule the tracking screen already applies to riders — unknown is its own answer and gets its own word, never a number nobody measured. Applied to the dish card and to RatingPill, which had the same bug waiting for whenever its callers are uncommented.

Verified by npx tsc --noEmit, npx eslint, npm run build and the ranking (60), platform, layering, switch, service-area and sw suites — all green. The ranking and matching logic in lib/search/dishes.ts and semantic.ts is behaviourally untouched: both passes changed how controls are presented and what the code says about itself, not what it selects or how results are ordered. Not verified in a browser — this environment has no headless browser, so the two sheets, the recent list and the “New” badge have not been seen rendering.
