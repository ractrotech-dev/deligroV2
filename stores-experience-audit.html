Stores experience audit

The customer Stores tab (/stores), read as a customer and measured against the live catalog. 14 September 2026.

What is actually behind the tiles

The Categories strip offers seven tiles. Counted against the 50 approved shops, using the same cuisine-tag rule the page filters with:

The cuisine tags actually in use across the catalog are Fast Food (35), Chinese (5), Biryani (3), Desserts (2), Italian (2), and one each of Healthy, South Indian and North Indian. The storefront taxonomy was written ahead of supply and almost none of it has anything behind it. Two further numbers set the scene: of 50 approved shops, 11 are open at the time of the audit, and exactly one has ever been rated.

Scenarios

“I need milk.”

Tap Dairy → “No dairy yet.” Tap Raw Meat → the same. Tap Chowpaty → the same. Three of the seven tiles are permanent dead ends and the strip gave no hint which, so the only way to learn was to tap each one and come back. The empty state itself is well written; it is being asked to do a job the strip should never have handed it.

“Which of these can I actually order from?”

39 of the 50 shops are shut. The list was sorted by delivery estimate alone, so closed kitchens were interleaved among open ones all the way down — and a closed shop’s ETA is a claim about a kitchen that is not cooking. You scrolled past shops you could not order from to find the ones you could, with nothing but a small badge to tell them apart.

“What’s good here?”

The “Open now” shelf was sorted by rating descending. One shop out of fifty has ever been rated, so for the other forty-nine that comparison is 0 − 0 and the shelf was whatever order the database happened to return — presented as a curated pick.

“Why is this word cut in half?”

The tiles were 68px wide with truncate on the label, which at 11px semibold clips the longest names the taxonomy has: “Pick & Drop” and “All stores” both lost their last word to an ellipsis. A tile whose entire job is to name a category should not be the thing that runs out of room.

“The selected one looks clipped.”

It was. The active tile is marked with ring-2, and a ring is drawn outside the element. Its container is overflow-x-auto, and that clips on both axes — there is no way to scroll one and let the other spill — so with no vertical padding the ring was sliced off along the top and bottom edges.

“Didn’t I just see these?”

The page showed an “Open now” shelf of eleven 72px avatars, then every shop again as a full card below it. The same eleven shops twice within one screen — once as truncated names under thumbnails, once as cards carrying the fee, the ETA, the distance and the closed badge.

What changed

The strip stops sending people into dead ends

The page now counts what each category actually matches and hands the strip the answer. Categories with nothing behind them sort to the end and render muted — still present, because an admin switched them on deliberately, but no longer dressed up as somewhere worth tapping. Groceries and Pick & Drop are exempt: they legitimately match no shops because they open their own screens.

The clipping, both kinds

Labels wrap to two lines instead of truncating, and the box reserves both lines whether or not the second is used, so every label sits on the same baseline. The scroll container gains py-1 -my-1 — the padding gives the active ring somewhere to land, the negative margin gives the space back so nothing below moves.

One list, split by the only question that matters

The avatar shelf is gone and the list is now Open now (11) followed by Closed right now (39), each sorted by soonest arrival. When nothing is open the page says so once, at the top, rather than leaving it to be inferred from a column of grey badges. The cards were always the better container — they carry the fee, the ETA, the distance and the closed state — so they do the whole job now.

Not done, and worth deciding on

  • Two different things called “Categories” (7). Home’s strip is cuisines, Stores’ is storefront types, and both carry the same heading on adjacent tabs.
  • The taxonomy is code (8). STORE_CATEGORIES is a constant. The admin can switch a category off, but adding one — or fixing that nothing matches “Dairy” — needs a deploy.
  • Membership is a string match (9). A shop is a bakery only if its free-text cuisines array contains “Bakery” or “Desserts”. Nobody is prompted for that at onboarding, which is the real reason four categories are empty — not that the shops do not exist, but that nothing ever tagged them.
